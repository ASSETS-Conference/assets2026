Accessibility Guidelines Guidelines for ensuring accessible submissions for ASSETS’26

Questions? Contact the Accessibility Chairs at access-assets26@acm.org.

Why accessibility matters

ACM publications reach a global audience that includes people with visual, auditory, motor, and cognitive disabilities. Making your paper and presentation accessible ensures that your work can be read, reviewed, and engaged with by everyone — including screen reader users, people who are deaf or hard of hearing, and attendees with mobility or cognitive differences.

Your responsibilities as an ASSETS 2026 author

  1. Submit an accessible PDF.
  2. Provide accessible closed captions for any video presentation.
  3. Make your live presentation accessible.

Part 1: Creating an Accessible PDF

Your PDF must be readable by screen readers and other assistive technologies. The following steps will help you check and fix the most common issues. For more information, refer to Adobe's accessibility resource center.

How to test your PDF

  • Check it is tagged: In Adobe Acrobat Pro, go to File > Document Properties > Description tab. Look for "Tagged PDF: Yes" in the advanced properties. Alternatively, try selecting text and pasting into a text editor — if this fails or produces garbled text, the document is likely not screen-reader accessible.
  • Run the accessibility checker: In Adobe Acrobat Pro, go to All Tools > Prepare for Accessibility > Check for Accessibility. Review each reported issue.
  • Check fonts are embedded: File > Document Properties > Fonts. All fonts should have "(Embedded)" after the name. Non-embedded fonts may need to regenerate the PDF.

How to fix accessibility problems

Fix problems in your source document (Word, LaTeX) where possible rather than in the PDF directly. On a PC, the Adobe plugin for Word exports accessibility features automatically. On a Mac and for LaTeX users, you will need to fix the PDF directly using Adobe Acrobat Pro or the free PAVE 2.0 tool.

Key fixes

  • Add document tags: In Acrobat Pro, go to All Tools > Prepare for Accessibility > Add Tags.
  • Add alternative text for figures: Right-click the figure > Edit Alternate Text. Describe the key information the figure conveys — not every visual detail.
  • Set the document language: File > Properties > Advanced tab > Language field. Set to English (or the appropriate language).
  • Fix table headers: In the Tags panel, ensure table header cells are tagged as <TH>, not <TD>.
  • Fix reading order: In Acrobat, use Reading Order panel to confirm text flows logically left-to-right, top-to-bottom.

Tip from the ASSETS community

  • A better base PDF is produced by using latex2pdf instead of ps2pdf for LaTeX documents.
  • Using the ACM LaTeX template correctly will handle many accessibility issues automatically.

Making Accessible Figures and Tables

  • Insert figures as images (PNG or SVG) rather than embedded PDFs — embedded PDFs can cause tagging problems.
  • Give every non-decorative figure meaningful alternative text that describes the key insight, not just "Figure 1".
  • Do not use images of tables — use proper HTML or Word tables with headers.
  • Give tables visible borders/lines to help with tagging.

Writing good alt text

Alt text should convey the purpose and content of the figure to someone who cannot see it. Focus on the key finding or message, not a pixel-by-pixel description.

Less effective More effective
"A bar chart." "Bar chart showing response times for three assistive technologies. Screen reader (blue) had the longest average response time at 420ms; switch access (orange) averaged 380ms; eye tracking (green) averaged 290ms."
"Graph of results." "Line graph showing task completion rates over 8 weeks. All three user groups improved, with blind users showing the steepest improvement from 62% to 91%."

For guidance on specific figure types, see the SIGACCESS guide to Describing Figures.

Part 2: Accessible Video Presentations

If your submission includes a video presentation, it must include accurate audio narration and synchronised closed captions. Videos without captions exclude deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers.

Key requirements

  • Do NOT burn captions directly into the video. Upload the video and caption file (SRT format) separately.
  • Video resolution: 1920 x 1080px. Format: MP4.
  • Manually review auto-generated captions before submitting — auto-generated captions are frequently inaccurate, especially for technical and accessibility-related vocabulary.
  • Keep your reading speed under 180 words per minute for clarity.

Accessible narration

  • Your narration should stand alone. Listen to your video without watching it. If everything makes sense, you are on the right track.
  • Describe all visuals verbally: graphs, tables, diagrams, and images. Say what the key finding is, not just "as you can see here."
  • Read or closely paraphrase all on-screen text rather than assuming viewers can read it.
  • Replace phrases like "as you can see" or "shown here" with explicit descriptions: "This graph shows..."

Caption formatting standards

Setting Standard
Font Sans-serif (Arial, Helvetica, Verdana)
Font colour White
Background Black background box behind text
Line length Maximum ~40 characters per line
Lines per caption Maximum 2 lines
Alignment Centre-aligned, lower centre of screen
Minimum display time At least 2 seconds per caption
File format SRT
Bottom clearance Keep the bottom ~1/5 of screen free of key visuals

For detailed captioning instructions, see the Adobe Premiere Pro caption workflow or the Flixier subtitles guide.

Part 3: Making Your Presentation Accessible

Presenters are responsible for making their live presentations accessible. ASSETS 2026 will have ASL interpreters and attendees who are blind or have low vision in the audience.

Please review the Accessible Presentation Guide prepared by Kyle Rector.

Key points

  • Describe all slides and visuals verbally. Do not refer to content without describing it.
  • Use high-contrast slide designs with readable font sizes (minimum 24pt for body text).
  • Avoid flashing or rapidly animated content.
  • Use a microphone — this benefits attendees who are hard of hearing and caption systems.
  • If you need any accommodations as a presenter (seated presentation, slide advancement assistance, extended time), contact the Accessibility Chairs in advance: access-assets26@acm.org.
