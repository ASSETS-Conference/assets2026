Session Chair Guidelines Guidelines for running an accessible session at ASSETS’26

Questions? Contact the Accessibility Chairs at access-assets26@acm.org.

Why accessibility matters

Session chairs help attendees follow presentations and take part in discussion. Planning for different access needs makes the session more welcoming and helps presenters share their work clearly.

Your responsibilities as an ASSETS 2026 session chair

  1. Check arrangements before the session.
  2. Support accessible talks and questions.
  3. Pause and seek help when an access barrier arises.

Part 1: Preparing Your Session

Use these checks before your session. Confirm local arrangements with the Accessibility Chairs and your session volunteer.

Before your session

  • Contact presenters in advance. Check name pronunciation and how they would like to be introduced. Invite them to share pronouns if they wish; do not require disclosure.
  • Ask privately about presentation support and preferred time signals. Agree any slide advancement assistance, seated setup or timing adjustments with the presenter and Accessibility Chairs.
  • Send presenters the existing ASSETS 2026 presentation guidelines. Confirm the talk and question timings, and encourage advance sharing of accessible materials through the organisers’ agreed route.
  • Identify your session volunteer and audiovisual contact. Confirm which interpreting, captioning and hearing support arrangements apply to this session, how to report a failure, and who will respond.

Check the room

  • Arrive with enough time to check microphones, slides and video sound with presenters and the audiovisual team. Check any caption display from audience seating.
  • Keep aisles, wheelchair spaces and access routes clear. Check the actual route to the presentation area and any agreed desk, chair or podium arrangement before the speaker needs it.
  • Check sightlines to the speaker, slides and any interpreters. Agree positioning and lighting with the interpreters and attendees who use the service; avoid darkening their area.
  • Agree how a volunteer will bring a microphone to questioners, including people who cannot raise a hand or reach a fixed microphone.

Part 2: Running Your Session

At the start

  • Introduce yourself and explain how questions will be taken, how to request a microphone and how to contact a volunteer privately for access support. Announce only services and locations that have been confirmed.
  • Remind everyone to use microphones, speak one at a time and identify themselves before speaking. Explain any agreed alternative way to submit a question.

Suggested opening words

“Welcome to this session. I’m [name], your session chair. Please use a microphone whenever you speak and say your name before your question. Our volunteer can bring the microphone to you. Please speak one at a time and allow time for interpretation or captions. If you need access support, please let me or a volunteer know.”

During talks and questions

  • Keep to the agreed schedule and preserve breaks. Use each presenter’s agreed time signals and allow time for interpretation, communication aids and responses during discussion.
  • If someone speaks without a microphone, repeat their question into yours before the presenter answers. Wait for interpretation to finish before moving to the next speaker.
  • Address the person directly, including when an interpreter or assistant supports communication. Ask before helping; do not move a person, mobility aid or personal equipment without consent.
  • If sound, captions or interpretation needed for participation fail, pause and ask the volunteer or audiovisual team for help. Contact the Accessibility Chairs if the issue persists. Explain the pause without identifying an attendee or disclosing their needs.
